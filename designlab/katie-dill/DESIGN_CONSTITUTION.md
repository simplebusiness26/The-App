## Experience thesis

Xplorer should help somebody understand what is available around them, trust what they are seeing, and move confidently from the screen into a worthwhile real-world experience. The app is therefore treated as one service journey rather than 76 unrelated screens.

## Experience model

1. Before — orient without demanding commitment.
2. Discover — explain why something is relevant, not merely that it exists.
3. Consider — make place/person/context legible before asking for an action.
4. Trust — preserve verification, privacy, audience and recency signals.
5. Commit — keep actions explicit and reversible where Product Truth permits.
6. Prepare — keep the map, route, conversation and event context connected.
7. Arrive / participate — let live state and communication do practical work.
8. Complete — do not manufacture extra engagement after the real-world task.
9. Remember / follow up — keep Moments, Memories, reviews and reputation distinct.

## Whole-app design decisions

- The existing semantic map palette remains intact; state colours are never reused as decoration.
- Persistent navigation remains complete, but is calmer and easier to scan.
- Map controls ask human questions instead of exposing filter mechanics.
- Discover makes the existing recommendation reason a first-class trust cue.
- Quick Access remains permission-correct but reads as grouped service areas rather than a raw route catalogue.
- Legacy theme consumers are brought back into the canonical Xplorer token system.
- The startup promise states the outcome of the service instead of listing content types.
- No route, table, RLS rule, permission check, account model, creation boundary or persistence rule is changed.

## Anti-imitation

This candidate does not reproduce Airbnb, Lyft, Stripe, or any other known product. Katie Dill's public design reasoning is used as a decision lens; the resulting interface is original to Xplorer and constrained by Xplorer's own Product Truth.
