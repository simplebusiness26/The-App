Stage One can't show a live city yet — check-ins and happening-now states are Stage Two. So this direction answers the question Stage One can answer honestly, and borrows its look from where local life is actually advertised: riso-printed flyers, gig posters and taped-up notice boards. The map base is quiet newsprint. The ink is spent entirely on the pins, because on this map a pin is not a dot — it's a state.

Pin states

  • Blue — it existsBusiness, property, park. Position and opening state.
  • Pink — something is scheduledClub session or event. The pin has a time attached.
  • Overprint — bothA place hosting something. The second ink sits misregistered, like a flyer printed twice. This is the signature move.
  • Yellow — an offer is runningManager-set, always time-bound, expires itself.
  • Dashed — unclaimedAn invitation, not an error. Someone can walk in and scan the QR.

Where this design disagrees with the brief

  • "Pins carry state" mostly needs Stage Two. Busy, live, taking bookings — none of that is knowable in Stage One. So the pins here carry scheduled state, not live state. Everything pink comes from an event or club page a manager typed in.
  • The core loop has no Join or Get There yet. Three of six steps are unbuildable now. Rather than grey out buttons, these screens end at Decide and say what actually happens instead: turn up, save it, walk over.
  • Reviews are the only feedback into the map in Stage One. If completed experiences are the metric, the verified-visit review is the only signal you'll have — so the QR scan needs to be the spine of the product, not a claims feature.
